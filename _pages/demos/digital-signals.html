Why digital signals resist noise

This interactive demo shows how digital (all-or-nothing) signaling — like action potentials — resists noise accumulation compared to analog (graded) signals. Each relay station adds random noise. The analog signal accumulates distortion at every step, while the digital signal is regenerated (compared against a threshold and restored to 0 or 1), discarding noise at each relay.

How it works: The error is the mean absolute difference between the original and final signal. For the digital side, since values are strictly 0 or 1, the percentage reflects the fraction of bits that were corrupted. With extreme noise, both errors converge toward 50% — the point where the signal is indistinguishable from random noise. This is essentially why neurons use action potentials (fixed-amplitude spikes) rather than graded voltage changes for long-distance communication.
