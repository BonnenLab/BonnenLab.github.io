Measuring the empirical horopter (nonius method)

Align two dichoptic nonius line segments at each eccentricity. The top segment is presented to one eye, the bottom to the other. Adjust until the lines appear collinear, then record. Your measurements are plotted against the Vieth-Müller circle (geometric horopter). Toggle the Hering-Hillebrand deviation to see how the empirical horopter flattens.

Nonius alignment

Horopter plot (top-down view)

How the nonius method works: Two thin vertical line segments are presented dichoptically — the top segment to one eye, the bottom to the other. At the fixation point, corresponding retinal points are stimulated and the lines appear collinear. At other eccentricities, the depth at which the lines appear aligned reveals the empirical horopter — the locus of points yielding zero retinal disparity.

H-H deviation OFF: Your simulated visual system has geometrically perfect corresponding points. Good alignment places measurements on the Vieth-Müller circle.
H-H deviation ON: The bottom line receives a systematic offset that grows with eccentricity — simulating the Hering-Hillebrand deviation. Even perfect alignment produces a flatter empirical horopter.

IPD = 63 mm · Viewing distance = 400 mm · H = 0.8 (exaggerated for visibility)
